Musings on Living

November 15, 2026 • Philosophy

When I am in a crowded space, there are moments when I wish to escape. I think about my favorite superhero, The Flash, and that prompts me to imagine running out of there without anyone being noticed. This was also why I gravitated to Xianxia novels during high school and the premise that there was a way to become omnipotent. This then led me to reflect on where I was currently in my life, and felt that I wasn’t truly living. I had plenty of hobbies and academic interests which I felt proud about, such as running a marathon and interning at NVIDIA in the area where I am interested. But this introspection brought me to realize I never really stood out in any particular area. I wasn't the smartest. I wasn't the fastest.

This naturally brings up the point of “comparison is the thief of joy”, but that also begged me to ask the question: what does it mean to live? Does living mean you are the best at what you do? One of my friends brought up the point that living meant creating something and finding fulfillment in the fact that you created something that could potentially impact other people’s lives or that you are the only one in the world who created something.

For most of history, humanity has existed in a state where our “living” was surviving till the next day. Whether that was our earliest ancestors living in caves hiding from apex predators, or farming to provide sustenance for their family. We have now come to a point in human development where most people don’t need to worry about such a way of life and so that brings me to question: What is the next evolution in what it means to “live”?

The common denominator between everyone before us was that the experience of living to survive till the next day was shared amongst everyone, regardless of who they were. This is what I consider living. No matter what stage we are at, the experience of living must be mutually shared amongst everyone regardless of who they are.

In our current world, there are two kinds of people. The person that contributes, and the one who escapes. These two things are not mutually exclusive. The creator puts stuff into the world to be experienced by people, and the escapee benefits off of the creator's creation to escape their responsibility of living. Examples of this are art, entertainment and drugs. My current thought on what humanity considers living is if you are putting out more content, whether that be quantity or quality, than you are consuming, you are living.

This is something I disagree with because of my previous idea of what living is. I am not sure yet on what living really is, and I myself am searching for the answer. The question is how do we go about searching for the answer? What experiences must we accumulate to know the answer?

There are clearly many different perspectives on the matter which we can see purely based on the wide variety of things people do. These perspectives range from the capitalistic west, to the spiritual east. I am not concluding that one is right and the other is wrong, I am just pointing out how many different approaches we are taking to get to the same place, whether we realize it or not.

At the end of the day, I think it is our responsibility as humans to do both introspection and extrospection on what it means to live and form their own conclusions. Everyone has biases that they form through their upbringing, who they surround themselves with, and the current state of the world, but I believe that we must look beyond them to what else is being explored to find the answer ourselves.
